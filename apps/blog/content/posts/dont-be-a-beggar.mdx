There's no shame in asking to be loved. It takes courage to let someone know where you're tender. But there comes a point when you're explaining the same hurt again, hoping a better sentence will make it matter.

In _The Idiot_, Myshkin thinks, "Compassion is the chief law of human existence." I want to keep that tenderness, and remember to include myself among the people who deserve it.

Someone can matter deeply to you and still be unable to meet you where a life together would begin. Understanding them won't make that hurt less, but it may let you leave without needing to make them cruel.

You can ask honestly for what you need, and let their answer stand. Their freedom to choose belongs beside yours to stop waiting.

I don't want to become embarrassed by how much I cared. There was something beautiful in being willing. I hope to carry that forward, with enough tenderness left to welcome a love I don't have to plead for.
