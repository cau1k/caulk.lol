Not everyone can ship code. This is the point I want to make in reference to DHH's recent post, ["Let the agents democratize open source"](https://world.hey.com/dhh/let-the-agents-democratize-open-source-9fd630a9). I think DHH has a fair point in terms of the broader ecosystem. But that's not to say a broken clock is right twice a day; however, I do think he often has subjectively correct takes with regard to the world of software.

But, not so much here.

I was never big into open source. I, and many, many others, have benefited greatly from it, but I was never an intense contributor to the "developers own the means of production" (sarcasm) side of things. Over the past two years, I have managed to reaffirm my passion for software through technology developed in the open, with goals to reach everyone and not just those willing to heft over an enterprise subscription. It's honestly beautiful. Never would I have expected to fall in love with a better implementation of RxJS (I'm talking about [Effect](https://github.com/Effect-TS/effect)). Nor would I have considered myself to (1) have the willpower, (2) be knowledgeable enough, and (3) extroverted enough to contribute my own work that millions of people benefit from every day.
